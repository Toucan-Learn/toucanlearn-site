# Toucan Learn offer review

25 October 2026. The offer has useful substance: a free first session and diagnostic report, then £40/hour with planning, weekly reports and founder oversight. The weakness is that visitors must assemble those facts themselves and cannot see enough evidence or practical terms. A bigger promise is not the answer.

## Book and business basis

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers*, Acquisition.com Volume I, 2021. The supplied books were not in the parent directory. Offers was found in Downloads as an EPUB archive named `.epub.zip`; its contents were a valid EPUB, not another EPUB nested inside a ZIP. A copy was renamed and converted with Pandoc to `../outputs/growth-review-20261025/100M Offers.txt`. The review draws on the book's pricing, value, offer construction and enhancement chapters, with the relevant chapters named below. The text conversion is kept outside Git.

The book is a framework for judgement, not evidence that these changes will increase Toucan's enquiries. Its examples concern very different businesses. Its four value drivers are useful here: a wanted outcome, confidence that the service fits, time before useful value arrives, and the effort required from the buyer. The short phrase “Effort & Sacrifice” is especially relevant to parents who already have enough administration.

The following seven changes are ranked. They are recommendations, not changes to the public offer or guarantees.

## 1. Remove invented proof before improving the sales wording

**Chapter 6: Value Offer: The Value Equation.** Perceived likelihood matters. Example testimonials presented among client reviews damage the very confidence they are meant to create.

Remove the four example figures at `index.html:298-329`. Check the original source and permission for the remaining three. Use the real Google review destination and one existing learner project, showing the actual task and work rather than turning it into an unsupported grade claim. An initial circle and adjectives such as “warm” are not evidence of tutor suitability; add verified scope and experience to each tutor profile.

This is the first change because better positioning beside dubious proof would make the problem worse. Do not commission more testimonial artwork while this remains unresolved.

## 2. Make the free first session a defined, useful first step

**Chapters 9 and 10: Creating Your Grand Slam Offer, Part I: Problems & Solutions; Part II: Trim & Stack.** List the actual obstacles and supply the smallest useful solution to each.

The parent needs to know: Will my child engage? Is this tutor suitable? What happens during the session? What will I receive? What happens if we do not continue? An adult needs the same clarity about their goal, current work and next step.

Use one consistent name, such as **Free first lesson and learning plan**. Explain the existing diagnostic report in plain language. Confirm and publish:

- The actual session length and who attends.
- What the learner needs to bring, with “nothing special” only if true.
- A short report containing the starting point, what was tried, what helped, and the proposed next learning steps.
- The real report delivery time and whether the learner keeps it without buying more sessions.
- That this is a tutoring assessment rather than a clinical diagnosis.

Do not silently change the length of an existing free-session commitment. A practical proposal is a short, reusable report format, not a bespoke assessment dossier. Set the delivery deadline from what tutors can reliably meet. The plan remains useful even if the family declines ongoing tutoring.

## 3. Say who this is for before describing the method

**Chapter 4: Pricing: Finding The Right Market; Chapter 6: The Value Equation; Chapter 16: Naming.** Specific audience and problem recognition make an offer easier to judge.

Keep Children and Adults as separate routes. Make the first parent message about a learner who needs a different pace or approach, including SEND-informed support. Make the adult message about the actual qualification, writing task, workplace English or practical skill. Do not make every visitor read a general explanation of psychology first.

Suggested factual copy, subject to the availability decisions in the site review:

> SEND-informed tutoring for children and adults, led by Joshua and the Toucan team. Online across the UK, with in-person lessons in London and Henley subject to tutor availability. Your first session and diagnostic report are free; ongoing lessons are £40 per hour.

This uses the supplied business description. It adds no grade promise. There is no basis here for claiming the market is underserved or that Toucan has superior results. Test whether the next five enquirers can accurately describe what is included, rather than running a statistically meaningless small-traffic A/B test.

## 4. Keep £40/hour simple and explain what it buys

**Chapters 3 and 5: Pricing: The Commodity Problem; Pricing: Charge What It's Worth.** Price should be assessed alongside the service, not as an isolated hour compared with every other tutor.

Show the same short offer near the first CTA on Home, Children, Adults and Contact: free first session/report, then £40/hour including planning and the weekly report. Where a parent asks about a weekly routine, four one-hour paid lessons cost £160; label that as an example, not a compulsory package.

If £40 is genuinely fixed, remove “What's your desired lesson rate?” at `contact.html:128` and synchronise the client/server field rules. It invites bargaining immediately after stating a fixed price. If concessions exist, describe the actual policy privately and clearly instead of pretending everybody receives an identical price.

Do not raise the price just because the book argues for premium pricing. First calculate delivery cost including tutor pay, planning, report time, founder review and any travel. At five clients, there is too little evidence here to justify an invented premium tier. The book's large price multipliers are not a Toucan forecast.

## 5. Make weekly reports the proof of ongoing value

**Chapter 6: The Value Equation; Chapter 14: Bonuses.** Existing work has little sales value if the buyer cannot understand it. Break down what is included without inflating the bundle.

The strongest distinction is already being delivered: a report after teaching and a founder who reviews it. Show one authentic, permission-checked, redacted example with four short fields: work covered, evidence of understanding, what still needs support, next step. If there is no suitable consented example, show an empty template labelled as a template. Do not pass the illustrative examples on Approach off as actual learner evidence.

Keep report production to the existing workflow. Do not add a portal, extra weekly meetings, unlimited messaging or extra assessments. Do not give the report a fictitious standalone value such as “worth £99”. The sensible application of Chapter 14 is to explain useful components; its aggressive bonus stacking is a poor fit here.

## 6. Guarantee controllable delivery, not learning outcomes

**Chapter 15: Enhancing The Offer: Guarantees.** A promise needs a clear trigger and remedy. High-cost personal services also need an affordable boundary.

The checked site says it does not guarantee grades (`children.html:59`) or grades, qualifications, jobs or immigration outcomes (`adults.html:58`). It offers a free first session and “flexible cancelling”. I did not find a positive money-back or outcome guarantee in the HTML. Do not review an imagined guarantee as if it is already policy.

Keep those outcome exclusions. The free session already reduces the initial financial risk. First decide whether the parent can simply stop after it with no further charge and what notice applies to later lessons. Say so plainly if that is the actual policy.

If a further guarantee is wanted, pilot a bounded service promise. **Proposed wording for decision, not publication:**

> If we miss the agreed report deadline, tell Joshua. We will send the report within two working days; if we cannot, we will credit £10 against the next lesson or return £10 if you are not continuing.

That example is measurable and concerns something Toucan controls. The deadline, amount, funding and treatment of existing clients must be agreed before use. Prefer the existing free first session over adding this promise until reporting reliability and cost are known. Never make the remedy conditional on a SEND learner achieving a grade or behaving in a prescribed way. Do not copy the book's punitive anti-guarantee language.

## 7. Remove effort and uncertainty rather than adding more services

**Chapter 6: The Value Equation; Chapter 9: Problems & Solutions; Chapter 10: Trim & Stack.** Remove low-value work and address the real friction before and after the lesson.

Use the existing enquiry routes. Ask for the learner's broad goal, level, preferred format and reply method. Agree the tutor and one of two real available times in the reply. Explain the first meeting and cancellation terms in the same message. Give an honest reply-time commitment only after confirming capacity. Avoid “book” language that implies instant booking when the actual action is an enquiry.

Do not build a scheduling platform or nurture funnel. Do not add free calls before every free lesson. Do not promise that every enquiry is a fit. Chapters 12 and 13 discuss scarcity and urgency; here, only real tutor capacity or a real exam date justifies urgency. A countdown or invented “two places left” would undermine trust.

## What Joshua must decide

2. Confirm whether £40 is fixed, and the actual cancellation and continuation terms.
3. Approve verified testimonial sources, tutor facts and any photos.
4. Confirm location coverage, tutor availability and the intended WhatsApp number.
5. Decide whether to keep risk reversal limited to the free first session. My recommendation is yes for now.

Do these before adding packages, bonuses or more website decoration. The first test is whether the next five serious prospects understand the offer and whether the booked first sessions lead to suitable paid work. Record objections in the existing enquiry notes; no new system is needed.
